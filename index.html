Model Revision 2026-09
What Changed And Why
Three structural corrections and one rewritten objective function.
A September 2026 audit re-derived the model's own balances from the calibrated state and found that three of them did not close. The closures below were rewritten rather than patched with compensating multipliers, and the model was then fit against the new objective on four measured cases. That refit still failed the benchmark gate hard — reduced χ² 8.48–29.30 and Durbin-Watson 0.115–0.256, in phase with the pour rhythm across all four cases. On 2026-09-27, three of the four cases turned out to have a pour-over video: frame-by-frame readout showed the hand-logged drained-volume column ran 13–73 mL high after the bloom. Refitting against the video liquid level — no equation, closure, or live parameter changed — dropped reduced χ² to 0.63 / 0.57 / 0.31 and pulled `k` / `tau_wet` off their bounds. A 2026-09-27/28 follow-up added the video server-temperature series to χ², aligned the stop-time operator on both sides, excluded readings after the dripper is lifted, and fixed two fitting-procedure bugs (the final-cup reading and the hydraulic-stage objective); after a full refit the canonical and kinu28/4:20 pass every gate, kinu27/4:12 fails residual whiteness only, and kinu29/4:11 (no video) still fails reduced χ² (8.37) and whiteness. No model equation changed in either step. See `README.md` → Video-derived measurements and Refit disclosure for the full list of caveats before quoting a number from this page.
Water inventory Absorption was tracked in two places that disagreed by 15.5 mL, and a level clamp created water out of nothing. Free water, mobile pore water, immobile pore water, and absorbed water are now four explicit states, each drawn down at most at its own content over a transfer time, so `V_poured = V_out + V_abs + V_imm + V_mob + V_free` holds by construction. Measured retained mass forces this split: a freshly wetted bed drains below the pore volume, yet the same bed holds more than pore plus absorption capacity at the end, so retention has to be a time-resolved quantity.
Thermal enthalpy The liquid-node balance dropped a term far larger than the ones it kept, and the enthalpy of the absorbed water — about a tenth of the energy input — entered nowhere. The node now carries `V_th = V_mob + V_imm + V_free + V_abs + V_equiv_coffee`, the inlet uses the full poured flow, and the liquid-dripper exchange is `U · A_wet(h)` with `U` bounded by a wet-paper-plus-ceramic series estimate. The previous lumped rate constant implied a `U` above that bound.
Extraction closure The internal-diffusion factor rose towards unity with time, i.e. the release rate accelerated as the grain emptied, and it suppressed most of the slow-pool mass; a large compensating coefficient existed only to undo it. The slow pool is now the first term of Crank's spherical-diffusion series, `λ_slow,i = π²D(T)/R_core,i²`; the fast pool takes the same first-eigenvalue structure over a sealed-inner-face shell, `λ_fast,i = π²D(T)/(2δ_i)²`, replacing the earlier Noyes-Whitney `A·D/δ` framing; and `D(T)` alone carries the temperature dependence for both. The pool split comes from measured shell accessibility per PSD bin, so the accessible solute mass is `dose · max_EY` identically and `max_EY` is frozen at its roast prior instead of being fitted.
Objective function The loss added five quantities with five different units, of which only two had any weight in practice, and one term was a smoothness penalty on the volume residual rather than an independent observation. It is now `Σ (residual/σ)²` over volume, stop time, extracted mass, and either the video server-temperature series or the single cup temperature, with stated σ values and log-space priors; retained mass is a gate, not a χ² term, because it is algebraically `poured − drained`. The hydraulic stages minimize only the hydraulic part of χ², so hydraulic parameters cannot absorb thermal error. That makes reduced χ², Durbin-Watson, and lag-1 autocorrelation (on r/σ) readable — and the unweighted residual is still correlated, not noise.
Full audit: docs/audit_2026-09-24.md. Change record and pre-refit diagnostics: docs/experiment_log.md, entries EXP-20260924-AUDIT-PHASE2, EXP-20260927-VIDEO-MEASUREMENT, and EXP-20260928-F12c-FIT-BUGS-AND-REFIT (with the F11 / F12a / F12b entries before it).
Project Showcase
Why This Repo Exists
A pour-over simulator that treats flow, temperature, and extraction as one coupled physical system.
Most coffee tools stop at recipe calculators or static brew ratios. This project instead tracks the bed as a changing porous medium and now ties that model back to measurement: real-time poured volume, drained volume, measured PSD, ceramic dripper heat capacity, and final cup temperature all constrain the same state trajectory.
Measured PSD The grinder is no longer represented by an idealized fractal PSD. Kinu 29 measured bins now drive D10, fines indicators, surface area, shell accessibility, and diffusion path.
Split Clogging Bed resistance now separates early throat blocking from later deposition, so `k_beta` is no longer one opaque lumped decay coefficient.
Bin-Resolved Extraction Each PSD bin carries its own `A_i`, `L_i`, `M_i`, and dissolved concentration, then recombines into the cup-side fast/slow output.

01   Flow Story
Hydraulic Mechanism
The brew is controlled by pressure head, bed resistance, and late-opening bypass.
This section is the hydraulic backbone of the model. The state does not assume one fixed flow path: water can go through the bed, be blocked by capillary and gas thresholds, or divert into wall-channel bypass once free-water head becomes large enough. The bed-resistance term is now explicitly split into early throat clogging and later deposition.
Drive `h(t)` and `Q_in` raise the pressure head. This is the immediate term that pushes water through the porous bed.
Resistance `k_eff` decays with fines migration, swelling, and compression, so the same recipe can move into a slower hydraulic regime.
Threshold `h_cap` and `h_gas(t)` subtract from usable head, which is why early brew flow can stay suppressed even with visible water above the bed.
Leakage Path `Q_bp` stays small at low head and opens later, so bypass is modeled as a head-activated parallel pathway rather than a constant loss.
Darcy extraction flow
Q_ext ∝ k_eff · h_eff / μ(T)
h_eff = h − h_cap − h_gas(t) + h_cap,wet
Split fines clogging
k_eff = k₀ / [ (1 + A_throat · (1 − e−V_out/V_char)) · (1 + β_dep · V_out) ]
early throat blocking saturates; later deposition keeps accumulating
CO₂ back-pressure decay
h_gas(t) = h_gas₀ · exp(−t / τCO₂)
τ_CO₂ ≈ 35 s　（Cameron et al. 2020）
Bypass flow (rib channels)
Q_bp = ψ · h_free · shape(h_free) · activation(h_free)
low water head ≈ no bypass; higher free-water head opens rib-channel flow
Particle swelling — Kozeny-Carman
φ(sat) = φ₀ − Δφ · sat
k ∝ φ³ / (1−φ)²　pore connectivity
In the generated diagnostics, this logic resolves into measurable outputs: water-head collapse, bypass spikes, permeability retention, and the point where the bed becomes fully wetted.
02   Extraction Story
Slow pool — Crank first-term release (per bin)
dMi/dt = −λi · Mi, λi = π² D(T) / (τ_tort · Ri²)
decaying flux; the previous exp(−L²/4Dt) factor increased with time, the wrong asymptote
Fast pool — bin-resolved Noyes-Whitney transport
k_ext,i(T) = A_i · D(T) / δ_i, δ_i = min(shell_thickness, R_i)
no Arrhenius prefactor: D(T) already carries the full temperature dependence
Bed concentration ODE — bin-resolved CSTR
dCi/dt = disi / V_liq − Ci · Q_ext / V_liq
Solid-phase depletion
dMi/dt = −disi
Temperature Dependence — Stokes-Einstein
D(T) = k_B T / (6π μ(T) r)
Fast solute radius0.40 nm
Slow solute radius1.00 nm
D ratio, 80 → 95 °C≈ 1.25×
The roast-dependent Arrhenius activation energies were removed in the 2026-09 revision: with a fixed-mobility D, an Arrhenius factor and a D inside the diffusion factor, temperature was being counted three times. Both radii are uncalibrated forward inputs.

03   System Map
Coupled Physics Architecture
Hydraulic, thermal, and extraction ODEs share one physical trajectory.
The model is organized around coupled state families, not independent post-hoc fits. Flow controls heat capacity, advection, residence time, and extraction transfer; temperature feeds back through viscosity, capillary retention, and the Stokes-Einstein diffusivity; measured PSD enters both permeability/clogging and bin-resolved extraction geometry.
Hydraulic ODEs `V_free`, `V_mob`, `V_imm`, `V_abs`, `w`, `V_out`, and `V_bed` evolve through Darcy flow, capillary retention, unsaturated `kr(S_mob)`, clogging, and channel-flow splitting. Their sum is a conservation identity, not a checked quantity.
Thermal ODEs Slurry node `T` and dripper node `T_dripper` exchange energy via full-inflow advection, an area-resolved `U · A_wet(h)` coupling, and ambient loss (`λ_dripper_ambient`); the cup-side observation layer adds vessel heat capacity and `λ_server_ambient` cooling. The liquid heat capacity includes the absorbed water. On video cases the server-temperature series (V ≥ 150 mL, probe immersed) enters χ², `U` is fitted and `λ_server_ambient` is frozen at a physical estimate of 3.7e-4 s⁻¹; with only a single cup temperature, `λ_server_ambient` is fitted and `U` stays at 194 W/(m²K).
Extraction ODEs Fast and slow solute states evolve per PSD bin and axial CSTR layer. The fast pool is a Noyes-Whitney shell transfer, the slow pool a Crank first-term release; both take their temperature dependence from `D(T)` alone.
This figure should be read as the model coupling contract: measured inputs stay fixed, hydraulic / thermal / extraction ODEs are integrated together, and fitting or benchmark outputs are projections of the same state trajectory.
04   Temperature Regime
Two-node thermal model
dT/dt = (Q_in/V_eff)(T_brew − T) − λ_cool(T − T_amb) − λ_ld(T − T_dripper)
dT_dripper/dt = λ_dl(T − T_dripper) − λ_da(T_dripper − T_amb)
Dynamic viscosity (water)
μ(T) = μ_ref · μ_rel(T) / μ_rel(T_ref)
μ_rel(T): empirical fit to the user-provided viscosity curve
Effect on flow rate
Q ∝ k / μ(T)
Lower T → higher μ → slower Q → longer contact time
Saturation concentration (Henry-type)
C_sat(T) = C_sat_ref · exp[α · (T − T_ref)]
Higher T → more soluble → faster EY build-up
The landing-page view here is not “temperature is good or bad”; it is that temperature changes both throughput and chemistry, and the slow bitter fraction reacts more strongly than the fast bright fraction.

This is the section that explains why fine grind does not just extract more. Once the bed compacts and permeability collapses, the whole brew migrates into a different hydraulic regime. The grind sweep below moves `k` explicitly through the Kozeny-Carman `d²` relation; `throat_clog_index` / `deposition_clog_index` (and therefore `β_throat` / `β_deposition` and `k_beta`'s PSD prior) are ratios of particle size to pore-throat size that are invariant to a uniform `psd_diameter_scale` shift by construction, so read this as "grind changes permeability", not "grind changes clogging severity" — the model does not currently represent the latter.

Technical Highlights
Model Surface
The repo exposes a reduced-order but fully coupled brew model, not a static recipe calculator.
The technical point of this project is that the brew is treated as a stateful system. Hydraulic resistance, thermal loss, accessibility, and cup-side dilution all evolve together. The current repo no longer maintains an older fractal-PSD branch; the measured-bin model is now the main line.
State Vector `V_free, V_mob, V_imm, V_abs, w, V_out, V_bed, V_poured, {C_fast,i, M_fast,i, C_slow,i, M_slow,i}, T, T_dripper, xi_pref`
Primary Outputs Brew time, cup stop time, bypass ratio, permeability retention, retained mass, TDS, in-cup EY, dissolved EY, water and energy balance residuals, and fast/slow cup balance.
Physical Closures Darcy flow, split clogging, Young-Laplace bed retention with mobile/immobile pore water, two-node thermal coupling through `U · A_wet(h)`, roast-dependent chemistry, and bin-resolved fast/slow release.
Core State
state = [V_free, V_mob, V_imm, V_abs, w, V_out, V_bed, V_poured, {C_fast,i, M_fast,i, C_slow,i, M_slow,i}, T, T_dripper, xi_pref]
Hydraulic Output Layer
Q_ext, Q_bp, k_eff, sat, h, brew_time, drain_time, throat/deposition clog diagnostics
Cup Output Layer
C_out, TDS, EY_cup, EY_dissolved, EY_fast, EY_slow, cup temperature fit error
